Microbes in Human Welfare for NEET: A High-Yield NCERT Chapter

By Sneha Iyer · 📅 October 08, 2026 · ⏱ 8 min read · 🎯 NEET Biology

Microbes in Human Welfare (NCERT Chapter 10, Class 12 Biology) is consistently tested in NEET, commanding 1–2 questions almost every year. Yet most students treat this chapter as "low priority" because it feels descriptive and application-heavy. That's a mistake. The NEET examiners love asking about fermentation mechanisms, the role of specific bacteria in biofertilizers, antibiotic production, and the science behind probiotics. If you understand this chapter with precision, you'll secure 2–3 marks easily while most competitors second-guess themselves. Let's break down what you need to master and the exact exam patterns you'll face.

1. The Foundation: Four Core Domains of Microbe Applications

NEET doesn't just ask "What are microbes useful for?" Instead, questions are specific and mechanism-driven. The NCERT textbook organizes this topic into four clear domains, and you need to know each one deeply.

Microbes in Agriculture: Biofertilizers & Biopesticides

This is the highest-yield subtopic. NEET regularly asks about nitrogen-fixing bacteria (Rhizobium, Azospirillum, Azotobacter), phosphate-solubilizing bacteria, and mycorrhizal associations. The key isn't just naming them—it's understanding the mechanism. For example, Rhizobium forms symbiotic nodules with legumes and fixes atmospheric nitrogen (N₂) into ammonia (NH₃) using the nitrogenase enzyme. Why? Because this reduces dependence on synthetic fertilizers and increases soil fertility. NEET loves asking: "Which microbe is used as a biofertilizer for rice?" or "How does Azospirillum improve crop yield?" Know that Azospirillum is free-living, not symbiotic, and produces growth-promoting substances like gibberellins and auxins.

Microbes in Food Processing & Fermentation

Fermentation is a favorite NEET topic because it combines microbiology with chemistry. Questions often ask about Lactobacillus in yogurt production, yeast in bread and alcohol fermentation, or Acetobacter in vinegar production. The critical detail: fermentation is an anaerobic process that produces energy (ATP) for the microbe while producing useful byproducts for humans. Lactic acid fermentation by Lactobacillus produces lactic acid, which preserves food and gives yogurt its tang. Alcoholic fermentation by Saccharomyces cerevisiae produces ethanol and CO₂. Know the glucose-to-product pathway for at least two fermentation types—NEET will test this as a fill-in-the-blank or mechanism question.

Microbes in Medicine: Antibiotics & Immunosuppressants

This domain directly impacts human health and is exam-heavy. Penicillium notatum produces penicillin (inhibits bacterial cell wall synthesis), Streptomyces produces streptomycin (inhibits protein synthesis), and Cephalosporium produces cephalosporins. NEET often asks: "Which bacterium is the source of streptomycin?" or "How does penicillin work?" Know that Fleming discovered penicillin by accident (Petri dish contamination), and this discovery revolutionized medicine. Also study Cyclosporine (from Tolypocladium) used in organ transplants—it's a common "match the microbe" question.

Microbes in Sewage Treatment & Bioremediation

This is lower-yield but still tested. Activated sludge treatment uses aerobic bacteria to decompose organic matter. Secondary treatment involves facultative anaerobes. NEET questions are usually straightforward: "What is the role of bacteria in sewage treatment?" or "Which stage of sewage treatment removes suspended solids?" Know that primary treatment is physical (sedimentation), secondary is biological (bacterial decomposition), and tertiary is chemical. Bioremediation using microbes to clean contaminated soil or water is also gaining traction in NEET papers.

🎯 High-Yield Exam Tip:

NEET examiners test mechanism, not just names. When studying Rhizobium, don't just memorize "nitrogen-fixing bacteria." Understand: Rhizobium enters legume roots → nodule formation → nitrogenase enzyme fixes N₂ → plant gets NH₃. This depth answers 90% of exam questions on this topic.

2. The NEET Pattern: Question Types You'll Face

Understanding the question distribution is crucial for time management during the exam. Based on the last 15 years of NEET papers, here are the patterns:

Pattern A: Mechanism-Based MCQs (Most Common)

Example: "Penicillin produced by Penicillium notatum inhibits bacterial growth by: (A) Inhibiting protein synthesis, (B) Inhibiting nucleic acid synthesis, (C) Inhibiting cell wall synthesis, (D) Inhibiting ATP production." Answer: (C). These questions test your understanding of how antibiotics actually work. Study the mechanism of action for at least three antibiotics: penicillin (cell wall), streptomycin (protein synthesis), and tetracycline (protein synthesis).

Pattern B: Application & Identification Questions

Example: "Which of the following is a free-living nitrogen-fixing bacterium used as a biofertilizer?" (A) Rhizobium (B) Azotobacter (C) Nitrosomonas (D) Nitrobacter." Answer: (B). These test whether you know which microbes are actually used in agriculture. A simple way to remember: Rhizobium is symbiotic (works with legumes), Azospirillum and Azotobacter are free-living (work with cereals and other crops).

Pattern C: Process Flow & Stage Questions

Example: "In sewage treatment, the stage where suspended solids are removed is: (A) Primary treatment (B) Secondary treatment (C) Tertiary treatment (D) Quaternary treatment." Answer: (A). These test your understanding of sequential processes. Study the three stages of sewage treatment in order: primary (physical, sedimentation) → secondary (biological, microbial decomposition) → tertiary (chemical, disinfection).

Pattern D: Match-the-Microbe-to-Product

NEET occasionally uses assertion-reason format or matching-column questions. Example: "Match the microorganism to its product: (i) Saccharomyces cerevisiae, (ii) Lactobacillus, (iii) Acetobacter = (a) Vinegar, (b) Bread, (c) Yogurt." Answer: (i)→(b), (ii)→(c), (iii)→(a). These require pure memorization, so flashcards are your best friend
