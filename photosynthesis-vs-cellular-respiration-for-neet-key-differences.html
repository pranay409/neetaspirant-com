Photosynthesis vs Cellular Respiration for NEET: Key Differences

By Arjun Mehta · 📅 November 28, 2026 · ⏱ 8 min read · 🎯 NEET Biology

Photosynthesis and cellular respiration are two of the most heavily tested topics in NEET Biology—and they appear across multiple question types: direct definition questions, equation balancing, comparative analysis, and diagram labeling. If you get confused about these processes or mix up their locations and equations, you'll lose marks that could've pushed you into your target percentile. This guide breaks down exactly what examiners expect you to know, with the precision required to ace both Biology Paper 1 and Paper 2 questions on this topic.

Overview: Why These Two Processes Matter for NEET

NEET Chapter 13 (Photosynthesis in Higher Plants) and Chapter 14 (Respiration in Plants) from your NCERT textbook are foundational—they typically account for 3–5 marks per paper when you combine direct questions, MCQs, and case-study sections. Many students confuse these processes because they're biochemically related: photosynthesis stores energy, respiration releases it. But their mechanisms, locations, inputs, and outputs are fundamentally opposite. Understanding this opposition is the key to acing every variant of exam questions.

Examiners often test your ability to distinguish between these processes using indirect clues: "Which organelle is most active during this process?" or "What happens to ATP levels when this occurs?" If you've memorized only equations without understanding the concept, these questions will trap you. Let's fix that.

The Core Equation Comparison: What You Must Memorize Correctly

This is where most students make their first mistake. They memorize the equations but don't internalize what each symbol means in context.

Photosynthesis Equation

6CO₂ + 6H₂O + Light Energy → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂

Breaking this down: Six molecules of carbon dioxide combine with water in the presence of light energy to produce one glucose molecule and six oxygen molecules. The critical point: light energy is consumed (input), and chemical energy is stored in glucose bonds. Oxygen is released as a byproduct—this is why photosynthetic organisms produce the oxygen we breathe.

Cellular Respiration Equation

C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + ATP Energy

Notice: glucose and oxygen combine to release carbon dioxide, water, and ATP energy. Here, chemical energy is released (output). This is essentially the reverse of photosynthesis—which is why some students think they're mirror images. They're not. The ATP produced powers every cellular function: muscle contraction, nerve impulses, protein synthesis, active transport.

🎯 Common Exam Trick:

NEET questions often ask: "During photosynthesis, light energy is converted to ___ energy." Students who've only memorized equations without understanding sometimes answer "kinetic" or "mechanical" instead of "chemical" (stored in glucose). Similarly, "During respiration, ___ energy is released." The answer is "chemical," not "thermal." Always think about energy transformation, not just molecular conversion.

Location, Organelles, and Cellular Context: Where These Processes Happen

NEET loves asking about organelle involvement because it tests whether you understand cell biology deeply. This is Chapter 13 and 14 territory, but it connects to Chapter 8 (Cell Structure and Function).

Photosynthesis Location

  • Primary Site: Chloroplasts (found in plant cells and some protists). This is non-negotiable—if a question says "eukaryotic cell" and "photosynthesis," assume chloroplast location unless stated otherwise.
  • Light Reactions: Occur on the thylakoid membrane (the stacked structures inside chloroplasts). This is where photons are captured by chlorophyll pigments.
  • Dark Reactions (Calvin Cycle): Occur in the stroma (the fluid-filled space inside the chloroplast). No light needed here—the name is misleading for exam purposes; it means "doesn't directly require light."
  • Prokaryotic Photosynthesis: Occurs on the photosynthetic membranes of cyanobacteria and some bacteria (not in a membrane-bound organelle). This detail appears in roughly 1–2 NEET questions per year.

Cellular Respiration Location

  • Glycolysis: Happens in the cytoplasm (cytoplasmic matrix). Two ATP molecules are generated here per glucose molecule. This is the fastest part of respiration.
  • Mitochondrial Respiration: The Krebs Cycle and Electron Transport Chain occur inside mitochondria—specifically in the mitochondrial matrix (Krebs) and cristae (Electron Transport Chain). The majority of ATP (~30–32 out of ~36 total) is produced here.
  • Key Difference: Respiration is not confined to one organelle like photosynthesis. This multi-location aspect is tested in diagram questions: "Label where ATP is produced most efficiently."

Exam Pattern: What Question Types You'll Face

Knowing where these topics appear helps prioritize study time. Based on NEET trends from 2023–2026:

Direct Questions (40% of marks on this topic)

  • "The light-independent reaction of photosynthesis is called ___ cycle." (Answer: Calvin)
  • "Which of the following is NOT a product of photosynthesis?" (Answer: typically CO₂, since O₂ is)
  • "Aerobic respiration produces approximately ___ ATP molecules per glucose." (Answer: 30–32)

Application/Concept Questions (35% of marks)

  • "In a plant kept in darkness for 48 hours, what happens to glucose and starch levels?" (Requires understanding that photosynthesis stops, respiration continues.)
  • "Why do plants respire even though they photosynthesize?" (Tests understanding that respiration is for energy, not just glucose storage.)

Diagram & Labeling (20% of marks)

  • Label chloroplast structures or mitochondrial stages.
  • "Identify where the Krebs Cycle occurs in this mitochondrial diagram."

Numerical/Calculation (5% of marks)

  • "If one glucose molecule undergoes aerobic respiration, how many CO₂ molecules are released?" (Answer: 6)
  • "In the light reactions, ___ NADPH molecules are produced per glucose." (Answer: 12)
⚠️ Mistake Students Make:

Many NEET students memorize that "photosynthesis = glucose production" and "respiration = energy release" but then freeze when asked: "Why do plant roots respire in darkness?" They don't realize roots have no chloroplasts and must respire continuously to survive. Practice questions that test conceptual depth, not just definition recall.

Strategy: How to Study These Topics for Maximum Retention

Three study methods have proven effective for NEET students tackling photosynthesis and respiration:

Method 1: Concept Mapping

Draw two circles—one labeled "Photosynthesis," one labeled "Respiration." Write all characteristics, equations, locations, and products for each. Then draw arrows showing opposite flows (energy in vs. energy out). This visual approach helps your brain encode the opposition and reinforces memory during timed exams.

Method 2: Equation Annotation

Don't just memorize "6CO₂ + 6H₂O + Light → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂." Write next to it: "Light source? Chloroplast? Product? O₂ released to atmosphere. Energy stored where? In glucose C-C and C-H bonds." This active recall approach during study forces your brain to retrieve and connect information during exams instead of just reading.

Method 3: Reverse Problem Solving

Instead of "What happens during photosynthesis?" ask yourself "If ATP is depleted in a plant cell, which process stops first?" (Respiration affects ATP directly, but photosynthesis wouldn't stop immediately.) These "working backward" questions mimic NEET's application-based MCQs.
